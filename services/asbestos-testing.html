Know what is in the material before work begins

Planning to remove flooring, disturb a textured ceiling, or renovate an older building? A1 provides asbestos inspections and sampling of suspect building materials so property owners and contractors can plan the next step with documented findings. We provide testing and consultation; we do not sell asbestos removal.

Appearance and building age alone cannot establish whether a material contains asbestos. EPA recommends professional assessment when suspect materials are damaged or will be disturbed. Avoid cutting, drilling, scraping, or collecting a sample yourself while the material is being assessed. Read EPA guidance for property owners.

Material analysis and air monitoring are different

Bulk material analysis investigates whether a sampled building material contains asbestos. Polarized light microscopy (PLM) is a common bulk-analysis method. Airborne fiber analysis may use phase contrast microscopy (PCM) or transmission electron microscopy (TEM), depending on the purpose and protocol. PCM fiber counts do not by themselves identify every counted fiber as asbestos.

Ask which method fits your project and which laboratory will perform the analysis. Laboratory accreditation applies to a laboratory's accredited scope, rather than being a blanket certification of every result. NIST explains the NVLAP asbestos laboratory accreditation program.

Common materials to discuss with an inspector

Flooring and adhesives, textured finishes, joint compound, pipe insulation, cement products, roofing, and siding may warrant assessment. Do not disturb suspected asbestos-containing materials. If you have vermiculite attic insulation, tell the inspector before anyone enters or disturbs it.

Requirements and timing depend on the project

Inspection, sampling, notification, and clearance requirements vary with the building, work, and jurisdiction. A single sample count or air-clearance number does not apply to every project. Confirm the requirements and inspection scope before scheduling demolition or renovation. Our guide to testing before renovation links to the relevant state programs.

Ask for the expected inspection and laboratory turnaround when requesting a quote. Sample type, laboratory workload, and any rush analysis affect timing.
