Understanding Your Environmental Testing Report

Learn what to look for in asbestos, mold, and indoor air quality reports, what results can tell you, and what to ask before scheduling.

Start with the question the inspection was designed to answer

A testing report documents the property areas inspected, the samples collected, and the findings within the agreed scope. It is not a test for every possible contaminant or a medical diagnosis. Ask which questions the proposed inspection can answer before approving the work.

What to look for in your report

  1. Property and inspection details. The date, inspected areas, relevant history, and conditions observed during the visit.
  2. Sample locations and identifiers. Each sample should be traceable to a documented location and to its laboratory result.
  3. Methods and results. The analysis method, units, reporting limits where applicable, and laboratory documentation for the work performed.
  4. Observations and limitations. Moisture readings, photographs where relevant, and areas or materials that were outside the inspection scope.
  5. Interpretation and next steps. A written explanation connecting the findings to the original concern, including whether further investigation or a specialist is appropriate.

Material testing and air testing answer different questions

An asbestos material sample investigates a particular building material. Air monitoring evaluates airborne fibres under the conditions and method used. A negative air sample does not establish that every building material is asbestos-free.

Mold investigations combine observations, moisture conditions, and sampling when it will help answer a defined question. An air sample is a snapshot; it does not by itself establish whether a building is safe for every person. EPA notes that there are no federal limits for mold or mold spores. Read EPA's explanation of mold sampling.

Preparing for your consultation

Bring the property city or ZIP code, approximate building age, any known leaks or prior work, the rooms or materials of concern, and your project deadline. If a contractor, insurer, school, or permitting authority needs specific documentation, share those requirements before samples are taken.

Independent testing and clear follow-up

A1 provides testing and consultation and does not sell remediation or removal services. We can discuss the report and the limits of the testing performed. Medical questions belong with your healthcare professional, and project-specific regulatory requirements should be confirmed with the appropriate authority.
